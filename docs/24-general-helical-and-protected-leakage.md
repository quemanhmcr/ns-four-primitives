# General Helical Magnitude and Protected Cross-Spin Leakage

The same-spin coefficient used throughout the project is one case of a fully symmetric magnitude formula for arbitrary input/output helicities. On the protected bi-monochromatic manifold, this general formula isolates the only pair interactions capable of reopening the optimal escape defect.

## 1. General helical coefficient magnitude

Let

\[
a+b=r,
\qquad
\alpha=|a|,
\quad
\beta=|b|,
\quad
\rho=|r|,
\]

and let the input helicities be `s_a,s_b` and output helicity `t`, all in `{+1,-1}`. Put

\[
A:=|a\times b|.
\]

For the normalization of `01-nonlinear-base.md`, the exact pair coefficient satisfies

\[
\boxed{
|C_{r;a,b}^{t,s_a,s_b}|
=
\frac1{2\sqrt2}
|s_b\beta-s_a\alpha|
\frac{A}{\rho\alpha\beta}
|s_a\alpha+s_b\beta+t\rho|.
}
\]

The previously used same-spin formula follows by setting `s_a=s_b=s`.

The four factors have distinct origins:

1. signed-radial mismatch `|s_b beta-s_a alpha|`;
2. incompressible area `A`;
3. dimensional normalization `1/(rho alpha beta)`;
4. helical triangle factor `|s_a alpha+s_b beta+t rho|`.

## 2. Protected bi-monochromatic state

Assume the optimal protected defect vanishes on the nondegenerate two-spin branch. Let

\[
\alpha:=m_+>0,
\qquad
\beta:=m_->0
\]

be the two protected shell radii. Then

\[
\Lambda u_+=\alpha u_+,
\qquad
\Lambda u_-=\beta u_-.
\]

The optimal multiplier is

\[
T=\Lambda-a_0-b_0\operatorname{curl},
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
a_0=\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta},
\qquad
b_0=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha+\beta}.}
\]

Its output symbols are therefore

\[
\boxed{
t_+(\rho)
=\frac{2\beta}{\alpha+\beta}(\rho-\alpha),
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
t_-(\rho)
=\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}(\rho-\beta).}
\]

## 3. Same-spin pairs vanish identically on the protected shells

Take two `+` inputs on the radius `alpha` shell. The signed-radial mismatch in the general formula is

\[
|\alpha-\alpha|=0.
\]

Hence their Lamb pair contribution vanishes for **both output helicities**. The same statement holds for two `-` inputs on the radius `beta` shell.

Thus, at a protected bi-monochromatic state,

\[
\boxed{
\text{all nonlinear forcing is generated by cross-spin input pairs.}
}
\]

This is an exact pairwise statement; no cancellation argument is involved.

## 4. Cross-spin pair formula

Take an input `A^+` of radius `alpha` and an input `B^-` of radius `beta`, with

\[
r=A+B,
\qquad
\rho=|r|.
\]

For a noncollinear pair, strict triangle inequalities give

\[
|\alpha-\beta|<\rho<\alpha+\beta.
\]

The two output-helicity coefficient magnitudes are

\[
\boxed{
|C_+(r)|
=
\frac1{2\sqrt2}
(\alpha+\beta)
\frac{|A\times B|}{\rho\alpha\beta}
(\rho+\alpha-\beta),
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
|C_-(r)|
=
\frac1{2\sqrt2}
(\alpha+\beta)
\frac{|A\times B|}{\rho\alpha\beta}
(\rho-\alpha+\beta).
}
\]

Both are strictly positive for every noncollinear cross-spin pair.

## 5. Exact protected-leakage coefficients

The pair contributions to the opening force `TF` have magnitudes

\[
L_+(r):=|t_+(\rho)C_+(r)|,
\qquad
L_-(r):=|t_-(\rho)C_-(r)|.
\]

Substituting the protected symbols gives

\[
\boxed{
L_+(r)
=
\frac{|A\times B|}{\sqrt2\,\rho\alpha}
|\rho-\alpha|
(\rho+\alpha-\beta),
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
L_-(r)
=
\frac{|A\times B|}{\sqrt2\,\rho\beta}
|\rho-\beta|
(\rho-\alpha+\beta).
}
\]

Hence for a noncollinear cross-spin pair,

\[
\boxed{
L_+(r)=L_-(r)=0
\iff
\rho=\alpha=\beta.
}
\]

Therefore an individual noncollinear cross-spin interaction is invisible to the protected-defect acceleration only in the special **equal-shell equilateral-radius geometry**

\[
|A|=|B|=|A+B|.
\]

## 6. One-shell hit still leaks through the opposite helicity

Suppose `alpha != beta`.

If the pair output lands on the protected `+` shell,

\[
\rho=\alpha,
\]

then `L_+=0`, but

\[
\boxed{
L_-
=
\frac{|A\times B|}{\sqrt2\,\alpha\beta}
|\alpha-\beta|\,\beta
=
\frac{|A\times B|}{\sqrt2\,\alpha}
|\alpha-\beta|>0.
}
\]

Likewise, if

\[
\rho=\beta,
\]

then `L_-=0` while

\[
\boxed{
L_+
=
\frac{|A\times B|}{\sqrt2\,\beta}
|\alpha-\beta|>0.
}
\]

Thus a cross-spin pair cannot hide merely by landing on one protected shell. The other output helicity at the **same wavevector** is pushed outside the opposite protected shell.

This is the protected-manifold analogue of the Spin-Shadow mechanism.

## 7. Acceleration / cancellation dichotomy

At a protected state, decompose the exact opening force into pair channels,

\[
(TF)_t(r)=\sum_{e\in\mathcal P_t(r)}x_e.
\]

Then

\[
\frac12\mathcal Y''
=\|TF\|_2^2
=\sum_{r,t}\left|\sum_e x_e\right|^2.
\]

Define at each output the opening composition defect

\[
\mathfrak D_{\rm open}(r,t)
:=
\left(\sum_e|x_e|\right)^2
-\left|\sum_e x_e\right|^2
\ge0.
\]

For any selected family of protected-leakage pairs,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{e\in\mathrm{sel}}|x_e|^2
\le
\frac12\mathcal Y''
+
\sum_{r,t}\mathfrak D_{\rm open}(r,t).
}
\]

Therefore a protected state containing many nondegenerate cross-spin pairs has only two ways to avoid immediate quadratic opening:

1. positive protected-defect acceleration;
2. additive/phase cancellation of the pair leakage.

The second branch is already in the domain of the Composition machinery developed earlier in the repository.

## 8. Exceptional equal-shell geometry

When

\[
\alpha=\beta=m
\]

and a noncollinear cross-spin pair also satisfies

\[
|A+B|=m,
\]

both output helicities lie in the same protected radius shell and the pair gives `TF=0` individually.

This is a genuine exceptional geometry, not an algebraic error. On the periodic lattice such equilateral-radius triads can exist. A full classification of zero-acceleration protected states must therefore treat networks assembled from these shell-closing mixed-helicity triads, together with possible cancellation networks for all other cross-spin outputs.

No claim is made here that those exceptional networks are stationary or globally regular. Their classification is the next rigidity target.
