# Quantitative Two-Rate Parabolic Control Cost

The exact pair-heat observability theorem identifies equal heat-rate classes as the linear cancellation kernel. This note gives a quantitative scale-invariant estimate for two classes whose rates are separated by a fixed fraction of the active parabolic scale.

## 1. Two class-resultants

At one fixed Fourier output, let two pair-heat class resultants satisfy

\[
\dot Y_j=-\nu\mu_jY_j+V_j,
\qquad j=1,2,
\]

where `V_j` is the nonlinear control generated by changes of the input modal factors. The observed forcing contribution is

\[
y=Y_1+Y_2.
\]

Assume exact cancellation at the initial time,

\[
Y_1(0)=A,
\qquad
Y_2(0)=-A.
\]

Fix an active frequency scale `N` and constants

\[
0<\delta\le M,
\qquad c>0,
\]

such that

\[
0\le\mu_1,\mu_2\le MN^2,
\qquad
|\mu_1-\mu_2|\ge\delta N^2.
\]

Take one parabolic observation window

\[
T:=\frac{c}{\nu N^2}.
\]

## 2. Free viscous separation

With `V_1=V_2=0`,

\[
y_0(t)=A\left(e^{-\nu\mu_1t}-e^{-\nu\mu_2t}\right).
\]

Put

\[
s=\nu N^2t,
\qquad
m_j=\mu_j/N^2.
\]

For `0 <= s <= c`, the mean-value theorem gives

\[
|e^{-m_1s}-e^{-m_2s}|
\ge
\delta s e^{-Mc}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^T|y_0(t)|^2dt
\ge
\frac{\delta^2c^3e^{-2Mc}}{3\nu N^2}|A|^2.
}
\]

Define

\[
c_0(\delta,M,c)
:=\frac{\delta^2c^3e^{-2Mc}}3.
\]

Then the free hidden cancellation becomes observably large on the natural parabolic time scale.

## 3. Add nonlinear control

Duhamel gives

\[
y(t)=y_0(t)+R(t),
\]

with

\[
R(t)
=\sum_{j=1}^2
\int_0^t e^{-\nu\mu_j(t-\tau)}V_j(\tau)\,d\tau.
\]

On `[0,T]`, Young's convolution inequality gives

\[
\|R\|_{L^2_t}^2
\le
2T^2\left(
\|V_1\|_{L^2_t}^2+
\|V_2\|_{L^2_t}^2
\right).
\]

Since `y_0=y-R`,

\[
\|y_0\|_{L^2_t}^2
\le
2\|y\|_{L^2_t}^2+2\|R\|_{L^2_t}^2.
\]

Combining with the free lower bound yields

\[
\boxed{
2\int_0^T|y|^2dt
+
4T^2\int_0^T\left(|V_1|^2+|V_2|^2\right)dt
\ge
\frac{c_0(\delta,M,c)}{\nu N^2}|A|^2.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^T|y|^2dt
+
2T^2\int_0^T\left(|V_1|^2+|V_2|^2\right)dt
\ge
\frac{c_0(\delta,M,c)}{2\nu N^2}|A|^2.
}
\]

## 4. Interpretation

For heat-rate classes separated by `delta N^2`, a cancellation of size `|A|` cannot remain hidden over one parabolic lifetime unless one pays in at least one of two scale-compatible actions:

1. **observed leakage action**
   \[
   \int_0^T|y|^2dt,
   \]
2. **nonlinear control action**
   \[
   T^2\int_0^T(|V_1|^2+|V_2|^2)dt.
   \]

The estimate is uniform in `N`; all dependence is through the dimensionless separation and window parameters `(delta,M,c)`.

This is the first quantitative time-integrated Composition estimate in the repository.

## 5. What remains

The theorem treats two separated class resultants. A general dense output may contain many heat rates, including clusters separated by much less than `N^2`. The next analytic step is a rate-cluster decomposition:

- separated clusters should be handled by observability/control estimates of the form above;
- a thin cluster of nearly equal rates should be retained as a near-resonant midpoint-sphere branch and passed to the complex Spin-Shadow plane-rigidity mechanism.

The remaining issue is also to bound the aggregate control terms `V_j`, which contain expressions of the form

\[
C_\alpha(F_a z_b+z_aF_b),
\]

by global or scale-local Navier-Stokes action budgets without losing critical scaling.
