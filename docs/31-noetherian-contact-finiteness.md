# Noetherian Finite-Contact Principle at a Protected Reset

The higher-order contact hierarchy leaves a potential Zeno loophole: perhaps a non-safe solution could touch the protected manifold to larger and larger orders, making every reopening flatter than the previous one. On the periodic domain, exact protected states live on finitely many lattice modes. This finite-dimensionality has a strong algebraic consequence: for each fixed pair of protected shell radii, infinite-order tangency is finitely determined.

This note uses only the Hilbert basis theorem plus the already established contact hierarchy. It does **not** provide a uniform contact-order bound as the shell radii vary.

## 1. Fixed protected shell pair

Fix two positive shell radii `alpha,beta` for which the periodic lattice shells

\[
S_+(\alpha)=\{k\in\mathbb Z^3\setminus\{0\}:|k|=\alpha\},
\]

\[
S_-(\beta)=\{k\in\mathbb Z^3\setminus\{0\}:|k|=\beta\}
\]

are nonempty. They are finite sets.

On the nondegenerate protected branch,

\[
\Lambda u_+=\alpha u_+,
\qquad
\Lambda u_-=\beta u_-.
\]

The protected multiplier is then fixed by the shell radii:

\[
T=\Lambda-a-b\operatorname{curl},
\]

with

\[
a=\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta},
\qquad
b=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha+\beta}.
\]

Hence `T` is independent of the modal amplitudes on this protected stratum.

## 2. Finite-dimensional polynomial phase space

Impose divergence-free and reality constraints and use the real and imaginary parts of the remaining helical coefficients on

\[
S_+(\alpha)\cup S_-(\beta)
\]

as real coordinates

\[
x=(x_1,\dots,x_d)\in\mathbb R^d.
\]

The periodic Navier-Stokes vector field restricted at the initial instant is polynomial in these coordinates: the viscous part is linear and the Euler convolution is quadratic.

Consequently, every time derivative of every Fourier coefficient at the initial instant is a polynomial in `x` (with coefficients depending on `nu,alpha,beta`). The same is true of every Fourier component of

\[
TF^{(j)}(0),
\qquad j=0,1,2,\dots.
\]

Although the Fourier support of `F^{(j)}` can expand with `j`, every component is still a polynomial in the same finite list of initial variables `x_1,...,x_d`.

## 3. The contact ideal

Let `P_{j,ell}(x)` denote all real and imaginary scalar components of all Fourier modes of `TF^{(j)}(0)`. Consider the ideal

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_\infty
:=
\langle P_{j,\ell}:j\ge0\rangle
\subset
\mathbb R[x_1,\dots,x_d].
}
\]

The polynomial ring in finitely many variables is Noetherian. Therefore `I_infty` is finitely generated. In particular, there exists an integer

\[
\boxed{N_*(\alpha,\beta,\nu)<\infty}
\]

such that `I_infty` is generated by polynomial components drawn from the finite collection

\[
TF,TF',\dots,TF^{(N_*)}.
\]

Equivalently, if a protected initial state on this fixed shell pair satisfies

\[
TF^{(j)}(0)=0
\qquad
j=0,1,\dots,N_*,
\]

then every higher contact polynomial vanishes as well:

\[
\boxed{
TF^{(j)}(0)=0
\qquad\text{for all }j\ge0.
}
\]

This conclusion is purely algebraic.

## 4. From algebraic infinite contact to dynamical persistence

The contact hierarchy in `26-protected-contact-hierarchy.md` shows that if all `TF^{(j)}(0)` vanish, then all time derivatives of the residual

\[
r=(\Lambda-a(t)-b(t)\operatorname{curl})u
\]

vanish at the protected instant. It also shows that all derivatives of the minimizing coefficients `a(t),b(t)` vanish there.

Strong periodic Navier-Stokes solutions are analytic in time at positive smooth times. On the nondegenerate branch, `a,b` are rational functions of analytic quadratic moments with nonzero Gram denominator, and are therefore analytic as well. Hence `r(t)` is analytic as an `L^2`-valued function near the reset time.

All of its Taylor coefficients vanish, so

\[
\boxed{r(t)\equiv0}
\]

on a time neighborhood of the reset.

Thus the protected manifold is not merely tangent to arbitrarily high order: after the finite algebraic threshold `N_*`, higher contact forces actual local persistence on the protected stratum.

Since `r=0` implies `kappa=0`, the critical balance there is

\[
K'=-2\nu M_3<0
\]

for every nonzero state. An infinitely flat protected contact therefore cannot initiate a positive critical escape.

## 5. Finite-contact alternative

For each fixed nondegenerate shell pair `(alpha,beta)` there is a finite integer `N_*` such that every protected reset satisfies one of two alternatives:

1. **persistent protected branch:** all contact jets vanish and the solution remains locally protected, so `K` decreases;
2. **finite opening branch:** for some `n<=N_*+1`,
   \[
   TF=\cdots=TF^{(n-2)}=0,
   \qquad
   TF^{(n-1)}\ne0,
   \]
   and the first nonzero defect derivative is
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal Y^{(2n)}
   =\binom{2n}{n}\|TF^{(n-1)}\|_2^2>0.
   }
   \]

So arbitrarily high finite-order flatness is impossible while the protected shell pair is fixed.

## 6. What this does to the Zeno loophole

The theorem is deliberately nonuniform: `N_*(alpha,beta,nu)` may grow as the shell radii and the number of lattice modes grow. This identifies the only remaining way for a Zeno chain to hide in contact order:

\[
\boxed{
\text{increasing contact order}
\Longrightarrow
\text{drift to protected shell networks of increasing algebraic complexity}.
}
\]

Thus high-order reset flattening is not a free dynamical parameter. It must be purchased by increasing finite-dimensional Fourier complexity. That branch is naturally coupled to the existing fragmentation / additive-composition program.

## 7. External input used

The only PDE input beyond identities already proved in this repository is the standard positive-time analyticity of strong Navier-Stokes solutions. Classical work of Foias and Temam establishes time analyticity for strong solutions; modern treatments give related analyticity statements in periodic and whole-space settings.

The Noetherian step itself is elementary commutative algebra: every ideal in a polynomial ring in finitely many variables is finitely generated.
